You may have a nagging concern, something that's been sitting on the to-do list, or simply wonder whether something your business has always done still makes sense.

You don't need to know the solution before speaking to us. The first job is working out whether there is actually a problem, whether it matters and what — if anything — is worth doing about it.

Commercial Risk

Sounds familiar?

  • We sometimes accept customer purchase orders without reviewing the terms.
  • We have standard terms and conditions, so we don't need to worry too much about which terms actually apply.
  • We would struggle to identify our largest contractual liabilities quickly.

Why might it matter?

The contract that applies is not always the one everyone assumes. Purchase orders, acknowledgements and terms exchanged after negotiations can create uncertainty. Notice periods, liability limits and insurance obligations are often overlooked until there is a dispute. How do you know that what you agreed commercially is what actually got documented?

What would we do?

We can help establish what happens in practice and identify simple process changes that create greater contractual certainty — without turning every order into a legal exercise.

Regulatory Boundary Risk

Sounds familiar?

  • We have introduced new products, services or ways of working since our last compliance review.
  • We think the lawyers looked at “it” previously, but we aren't entirely sure exactly what they signed off.
  • We know the regulations that apply to our core business, but have never really considered whether our smaller or newer activities fall somewhere different.

Why might it matter?

“We had legal advice on that” can sometimes mean something was discussed rather than formally reviewed with clear written advice. Businesses evolve and business law changes regularly, so good historic advice may no longer answer today's question.

What would we do?

We can help establish what was actually considered, what has changed and whether there is genuinely a gap. Sometimes the existing advice is enough; if it isn't, we can identify the specific question that needs answering.

Financial Crime & Sanctions

Sounds familiar?

  • We trade internationally or have overseas customers and suppliers.
  • We don't routinely consider sanctions when starting new business relationships.
  • Anti-money laundering (AML) regulations don't apply to us, so we have assumed wider financial-crime requirements probably don't either.

Why might it matter?

Financial-crime risk extends beyond businesses formally subject to AML regulation. You may also be signing contracts stating that you undertake a higher level of compliance checking than you actually do.

What would we do?

We can help management understand the basic framework and identify where your business could realistically encounter risk. Sometimes awareness and sensible checks are the proportionate response.

Competition Law

Sounds familiar?

  • Our people meet competitors through trade associations, industry events or ordinary business relationships.
  • We operate in a relatively small industry where competitors know one another well.
  • “It's a small industry — there are only a few of us and we have to work together.”
  • We have never given employees practical guidance about what they can and cannot discuss with competitors, especially about pricing.

Why might it matter?

Competition problems can arise from informal conversations as well as formal agreements. Something described as “industry practice” isn't necessarily lawful, while many legitimate interactions cause unnecessary concern because nobody understands the boundary.

What would we do?

We can help people understand where that boundary sits, recognise conversations that require care and know when specialist advice is actually needed.

Cyber & Information Governance

Sounds familiar?

  • We have introduced AI tools — or know or suspect that some employees are using them — in the last 12 months.
  • We rely heavily on third-party software and cloud providers, so we don't need to worry too much about it.
  • IT manages our cyber risk, so the Board doesn't need to understand much of the technical detail.

Why might it matter?

New technology can change information flows without anyone consciously deciding to change them. Outsourcing technology does not necessarily outsource the business risk. The first question is understanding what information you have, where it goes and who can access it.

What would we do?

We can help map practical information flows, identify where the real risks sit and distinguish things that need action from those that simply need to be understood.

Key Person Risk

Sounds familiar?

  • Important business knowledge sits with one or two people and we have never discussed the risks.
  • Some key processes work because particular individuals are experts and just make them work.
  • A major customer, supplier or adviser relationship depends heavily on one person.

Why might it matter?

Key-person risk isn't only about somebody leaving. Holidays, illness, promotion or rapid growth can expose dependencies that were invisible while everything was working normally. A future buyer will also focus on these dependencies and it can affect the terms they are prepared to put forward.

What would we do?

We can identify the dependencies that genuinely matter and proportionate ways of reducing them now, or when you are ready — without trying to document everything everybody knows.

Insurance & Claims

Sounds familiar?

  • We have never talked about our insurance arrangements as a programme; a broker just sorts it out for us and we don't have to do much.
  • Don't worry about missing disclosure — we can say they never asked us about it.
  • We don't have a clear picture of our largest uninsured or potentially uninsured exposures.
  • We have never heard the expression “circumstances which might give rise to a claim”.

Why might it matter?

Renewing insurance is not the same as reviewing risk. Gaps regularly emerge between the risks management thinks have been transferred, and what policies actually cover. Exactly when you notify a circumstance or claim can also have a material effect on whether your policy pays out.

What would we do?

We can look at insurance from the business-risk side: identifying exposures worth testing, strengthening renewal search and disclosure processes, and working with brokers or specialist advisers to close gaps and reduce worry.

Transaction Readiness

Sounds familiar?

  • We may seek investment, refinancing or a sale in the next few years.
  • Some historic decisions, contracts or records would take time to locate and explain.
  • We have generally been informal in how we record decisions.

Why might it matter?

Good transactions build momentum. Searching for records, reconstructing decisions and discovering issues during due diligence will interrupt it. Every process will identify issues but the objective is for surprises to be fewer, smaller and less material – early preparation is never wasted.

What would we do?

We can identify what is worth organising or fixing now, what can wait and what simply needs explaining — so future due diligence supports transaction momentum rather than interrupting it.

Recognise something?

These aren't a compliance checklist, and recognising something doesn't necessarily mean you have a problem.

The experienced support we provide is about listening carefully, asking the right questions, recognising patterns, ruling things out and deciding what actually matters.

Sometimes something needs fixing. Sometimes it needs monitoring. Sometimes specialist advice is required. Sometimes the right answer is that nothing more needs to be done for now.
